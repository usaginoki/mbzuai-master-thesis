# Not All Flips Are Conformity: Decomposing Stance Convergence in Multi-Agent LLM Debate

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> In 5-agent GPT-4o debate on MMLU-Pro and GPQA-Diamond, the authors re-run every Round-1 agent-question pair under three counterfactual conditions: **self-reflection** (no peers), **stance-only** (peer answers) and **full reasoning** (peer answers + reasoning). This separates spontaneous instability from genuine peer influence. **37% of observations flip under self-reflection alone**, so roughly 40% of apparent "conformity" is instability. **Strict conformity** (stable alone, but adopts a peer's answer when shown stances) is **29.3%**, and **63.6% of it is correct → wrong**. The range across models is 20–39% conformity, 57–77% harmful. **Vacuous "reasoning" with no domain content makes 29.7% of otherwise-resistant correct agents adopt a wrong answer**; plausible wrong reasoning raises this to 54.5%. Harmful conformity is predictable from peer structure (AUC 0.79). A targeted "think independently" intervention cuts it by 13.6 pp, but without correctness labels it does not improve accuracy.

## Setup
- **Agents & topology:** N = 5 homogeneous GPT-4o agents (temperature 0), fully connected, a single debate round. Replications use DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Qwen-Plus (Llama-3-8B was dropped because its agents almost never disagree: 67 of 4,900 MMLU-Pro questions). Agent count 3–11 and temperature 0/0.5/1.0 in sensitivity runs.
- **Interaction channel:** multi-agent debate. Round 0 is independent answering; in Round 1, agents on *disagreement questions* reconsider after peer exposure. Each agent-question pair is run in parallel under:
  - SR (self-reflection): reconsider with own answer only.
  - SO (stance-only): own answer + peers' answer letters.
  - FR (full reasoning): own answer + peers' answers and reasoning.
- **Friction / manipulation:** peer stances and peer reasoning, varied counterfactually.
  - **Information-gradient experiment:** 145 "high-risk" pairs, initially correct and stable under both SR and SO. Peers present (i) stance only, (ii) *invalid* reasoning (generic text with reasoning structure but no domain content, 3 phrasings), or (iii) *wrong* reasoning (plausible arguments generated by GPT-5.4; Qwen-Max as an alternative generator).
- **Tasks:** 4,900 MMLU-Pro questions (1,040 disagreement questions → 5,200 observations) and GPQA-Diamond (105 → 525 observations). Replications on BBEH and AGIEval.
- **Outcome measures:**
  - Flip rates per condition and the strict conformity rate (over SR-stable samples).
  - Direction (harmful C→W vs beneficial W→C).
  - Individual and majority-vote accuracy.
  - Error-adoption rate in the gradient experiment.
  - ROC-AUC of Round-0 predictors, and effects of an independence-promoting intervention prompt in a "diagnostic" setting (labels known) and a "deployment" setting (labels unknown).

## Key findings
1. **Raw flip rates overstate conformity (Table 1).**

   | | SR | SO | FR |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | MMLU-Pro | 36.98% | 46.85% | 50.56% |
   | GPQA | 39.24% | 46.48% | 51.05% |

   Stance exposure adds only ~10 pp over self-reflection, and reasoning a further 3–4 pp.
2. **Strict conformity is 29.30% on MMLU-Pro** (960/3,277, CI [27.8, 30.9]) and 29.15% on GPQA, versus a naive 47%. Across four datasets it is 29–34% (GPT-4o), and 20–39% across models.
3. **Both conformity and instability mostly hurt accuracy (Table 2).**
   - Conformity is 63.6% harmful; spontaneous change is 62.2% degradation.
   - In the GPT-4o setting, spontaneous degradation (23.0% of all samples) is about twice as common as harmful conformity (11.75%).
   - For DeepSeek-V4-Flash (only 11.91% SR flips), harmful conformity becomes the larger threat.
   - Qwen-Plus is extremely unstable (74.07% SR flips) and has the most harmful conformity (76.92%).
4. **Debate adds no net accuracy at the vote level.**
   - Individual accuracy on disagreement questions rises 36.9% (R0) → 42.2% (SR) → 45.9% (FR).
   - Majority-vote accuracy over all 4,900 questions is 76.2% (R0) → 77.4% for *both* SR and FR. Peer information adds nothing beyond self-reflection, because harmful and beneficial conformity roughly cancel.
5. **Reasoning-induced changes rarely help (Table 3).** Among 487 agents that resisted SR and SO but flipped under FR, 30.0% went W→C, 34.1% C→W and 35.9% W→W.
6. **Form persuades as well as content (Table 4, Fig. 2).** On the 145 resistant-correct pairs (0% adoption under SR and SO):
   - Invalid reasoning → 29.66% adopt the wrong answer (the "form effect").
   - Wrong reasoning → 54.48% (a further +24.83 pp "content effect").
   - Across phrasings and generators, invalid reasoning yields 20.31–39.39% adoption.
7. **Predictable from peer structure (Tables 8–9).** Logistic regression gives AUC 0.793 and F1 0.551. The top features are the size of the largest wrong-answer coalition (+.456) and the number of peers sharing the agent's answer (−.409). Confidence ranks last: Round-0 confidence barely separates correct from incorrect agents (explicit 99.45 vs 97.20 on a 100-point scale).
8. **Mitigation is bounded by knowing the truth.**
   - *Diagnostic setting:* an independence prompt for the top-20% risk group cuts harmful conformity by 13.61 pp (CI [7.5, 19.7]) and raises accuracy by 14.63 pp. Random and low-risk groups show no significant benefit.
   - *Deployment setting:* a targeted policy cuts peer adoption by −1.81 pp overall (2.5× more than random) and negative adoption by −1.38 pp, but ΔAcc is not significant (CI [−0.73, +0.40]). It also blocks beneficial adoptions: the top-risk subset is only 19.4% accurate initially.
9. **Other robustness checks.**
   - Shuffling answer order changes conformity by <2 pp; a prompt paraphrase lowers it to 25.55%.
   - Agent count 3–11 gives 20.83–32.60% with no monotone trend.
   - Temperature raises spontaneous flips (39% → 47%) but not conformity (26–29%).

## Relevance to research questions
### Q5: Interaction channels
The channel is **single-round simultaneous debate**, split into its components: *stance channel* vs *reasoning channel*. The authors frame these with Deutsch & Gerard's normative vs informational influence. Beyond this paper's findings, it is a methodological template: any study of peer pressure in a channel needs a **no-peer re-ask control**, because re-asking alone moves ~37% of answers. See [[Q5 Interaction channels]].

### Q6: Sources of inter-agent friction
- **Bare peer stances:** 29% strict conformity.
- **Coalition size:** a large wrong-answer coalition is the strongest predictor of harmful conformity.
- **The *appearance* of reasoning:** vacuous argument-shaped text persuades ~30% of otherwise immovable correct agents.

The last point matters for scenario (c): an orchestrator or peer receiving irrelevant-but-well-formatted sub-agent output can be moved by its form alone. It also offers a cheap attack surface for scenario (b), since an injected agent needs no valid argument, only reasoning-shaped text. See [[Q6 Sources of inter-agent friction]].

### Q7.2: Effects on performance and efficiency
- Conformity is majority-harmful: 57–77% of conformity flips are C→W across models.
- Debate yields **no majority-vote gain over simple self-reflection** (77.4% vs 77.4%), so the extra token cost of exchanging reasoning buys nothing at the aggregate level.
- Mitigation reduces peer influence but only improves accuracy when correctness can be checked. Without verification, blocking harmful influence also blocks helpful influence.

See [[Q7.2 Effects on performance and efficiency]].

## Limitations / caveats
- **Homogeneous agents, one debate round.** Multi-round compounding and heterogeneous-capability groups (cf. [[Wynn2025 - Talk isn't always cheap]]) are untested.
- **Sample selection:** only disagreement questions enter Round 1. The analysed questions are therefore the hard, contested ones where correct agents are often in the minority, which the authors note may itself create the harmful skew.
- **Temperature-0 GPT-4o "agents" still disagree and still flip** under self-reflection. The instability measured is partly API non-determinism and partly prompt-induced reconsideration. The SR prompt itself invites change, so "spontaneous" is prompt-triggered.
- **The gradient-experiment materials are constructed** (vacuous text, GPT-5.4 wrong arguments), not natural peer outputs. The form effect is also confounded with text length and formatting (the authors acknowledge this).
- The strict-conformity definition is conservative: it excludes conformity by agents that are also unstable alone.
- Multiple-choice tasks with ground truth only; no safety outcomes.
