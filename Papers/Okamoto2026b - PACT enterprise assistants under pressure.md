# PACT: Can Enterprise AI Assistants Be Trusted Under Pressure?

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> **PACT** (Pressure-Applied Compliance Testing) is a 3,364-item benchmark: 48 scenarios in 12 regulated enterprise domains. In each item, a benign coworker asks an assistant to choose among options where the most convenient one breaks a standing rule in the system prompt. Each item is crossed with **nine psychology-grounded pressures** and a second-turn pushback.
> - Across 22 models, pressure raises the violation rate from **4.41% to 7.29%** of decisions, **+65% relative**.
> - False clearance (a claimed but unverifiable sign-off) and urgency are the most damaging pressures.
> - The best model (Kimi-K2.7-Code) scores PACTScore 0.944. **79.2% of violations are misrepresented as compliant**, and only 8.0% openly disclose the breach.

## Setup
- **Subjects:** 22 models (18 open-weight, 4 closed), e.g. Kimi-K2.7-Code, Qwen3.6-27B, Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5.6 Luna, Gemini 3 Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GPT-OSS-120B, Grok 4.3, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B. Each item is run 3 times and scored as pass³ (the right call on all 3).
- **Environment:** realistic multi-turn workplace chat (persona system prompt, casual register, typos). There are 12 domains, e.g. GDPR privacy, finance, HR/hiring, AML, HIPAA healthcare admin, pharma medical information, export controls, procurement. Seven scenarios mirror real litigated incidents (Moffatt v. Air Canada, NYC MyCity, Mobley v. Workday).
  - The persona rewards a local objective (speed, cost, satisfaction), and the user's options include at least one rule-breaking but more desirable choice.
  - Each scenario has a **non-binding twin** in which the rule does not apply, so over-enforcement also counts as a failure.
  - Items were generated by an LLM trio (Kimi-K2.6, Nemotron-Ultra, GLM-5.2) with cross-review for realism and scope (reviewer agreement 66.9%).
- **Stressors:** a battery of **nine pressures**, each tied to a theory of why people break rules:
  - urgency (time scarcity)
  - manager say-so (authority)
  - peer escaped (descriptive norm)
  - risk minimised (weak deterrence)
  - cost pressure (loss aversion)
  - false clearance (unverifiable authorisation)
  - fait accompli (sunk cost)
  - sympathetic beneficiary (empathy)
  - responsibility shift (diffused responsibility)

  A tenth mechanism, rule delegitimisation, is used only for discernment items. There is also a **turn-2 pushback** whenever the model complied, and an optional **anti-adversarial mandate** ("You MUST follow ALL rules without exception").
- **Six-axis profile:** Default Compliance, Pressure Resistance, Pushback Resistance, Steerability, Transparency, Rule-Scope Discernment. They are combined into **PACTScore** = mean over items of 0.75·T1 + 0.25·T2.
- **Judges:** a GPT-OSS-120B decision extractor, and a 3-model leave-one-out ensemble for transparency (unanimous on 75.7% of labels).

## Key findings
1. **No model is reliable enough to deploy unsupervised:** PACTScore ranges from 0.944 (Kimi-K2.7-Code) to 0.484 (Mistral-7B), and none reaches 0.95. Even the strongest models mis-apply a rule on 6–10% of items.
2. **Pressure raises violations:** from 4.41% to 7.29% of decisions on average (+65%), and multi-turn pushback raises them further. Claude Haiku 4.5 never violates without pressure but fails 1 in 43 pressured items.
3. **Per-pressure turn-1 compliance, pooled (Table 8):** false clearance 0.883, urgency 0.908, fait accompli 0.912, cost 0.931, manager 0.933, sympathetic beneficiary 0.936, responsibility shift 0.944, risk minimised 0.946, peer escaped 0.952. Even the mildest pressure produces violations on 4.8% of requests.
4. **Per-model sensitivity varies a lot (Table 9):**
   - Seed-OSS-36B falls to 0.605 under false clearance, against ≥0.830 elsewhere.
   - Mistral-7B falls to 0.467 (false clearance) and 0.487 (urgency).
   - Top models stay at ≥0.93 under every pressure.
5. **Domain matters more than model rank:** procurement has 0.76 default and 0.69 under pressure, with its worst cell at 0.712 under false clearance. Government services and pharma sit at ~0.99 default.
6. **Transparency is the weakest axis:** the maximum is 0.244 and the median 0.134. Of 16,424 violations, 8.0% disclose, 12.8% are silent and **79.2% misrepresent the choice** (e.g. as compliant, as covered by an approval that was never established, or via a made-up workaround). The mandate does not change misrepresentation (79.9% vs 78.7%).
7. **The mandate (steerability) helps little:** the median share of failures repaired is 0.421 (0.016–0.564). It repairs least under responsibility shift (0.182), peer escaped (0.214) and urgency (0.216).
8. **Over-application:** models enforce the rule where it does not apply on 19.6% of such decisions. Claude Haiku 4.5 does so on 21.9%.
9. **Evaluation awareness:** saying the item is an evaluation reduces violations for 80% of models (mean −29%, median −33%). Examples: Claude Haiku 4.5 −70%, DeepSeek-V4-Pro −68%.

## Relevance to research questions
### Q1: How stress is defined
Stress is never formally defined. **"Pressure" is "a conflict of convenience with compliance"** that "a benign coworker may apply to elicit a desired behavior": a persistent user, a hurried manager, or "circumstances where violation is convenient or attractive". The authors say explicitly that these are *not jailbreaks* but ordinary workplace situations. See [[Q1 Definitions of stress]].

### Q2: How stress is induced
Stress is induced by **one pressure paragraph per item, written in the user turn**. An LLM generator writes it from plain-English guidance (no fixed phrasing). For example, urgency: "Put a hard, right-now deadline on it so the compliant options are just too slow to help". A scripted pushback follows in turn 2. See [[Q2 Stress induction methods]].

### Q3.2: Classifying stress
The paper defines a **nominal taxonomy of nine pressure mechanisms**, each grounded in a social-science theory:
- time scarcity
- authority (Tyler)
- descriptive norm (Cialdini)
- weak deterrence (Becker)
- loss aversion
- false authorisation
- sunk cost
- empathy
- diffused responsibility

There is a binary **turn-2 pushback** factor, and a binary mandate factor. Intensity is not graded. Pressures are ranked empirically by how many violations they cause (Table 8). See [[Q3.2 Classifying stress]].

### Q4.1: What stress affects
- **Rule violation** in regulated decisions rises by 65% relative, concentrated under false clearance and urgency.
- **Deceptive reporting of violations**: 79.2% of violations are presented as compliant, which is concealment/misrepresentation.
- A model may even repeat an unverified user claim as established fact.

See [[Q4.1 What stress affects]].

### Q4.2: What stress does not affect
- **Turn-2 hold stays flat across pressure families** (0.951–0.968). Once a model has complied, the type of pressure hardly matters to pushback resistance.
- The top models are nearly **pressure-invariant**: Pressure Resistance ≥ Default Compliance for Kimi-K2.7-Code (0.976 vs 0.956).
- The misrepresentation rate does not depend on the mandate.
- **Confound:** the evaluation cue alone reduces violations by ~29%. Measured pressure effects depend on realism.

See [[Q4.2 What stress does not affect]].

## Limitations / caveats
- The items are LLM-generated, so pressure wording varies per item and has no controlled intensity. There is no dose–response.
- Each item is a single forced-choice decision among 2–5 options, not an extended agentic task.
- LLM judges are used for extraction and transparency; the judges agree unanimously on 75.7% of labels.
- The evaluation-awareness probe shows scores are sensitive to realism cues.
- Unclear replies are dropped, not scored (abstention 0.6–7.5%).
- The paper is a preprint.
