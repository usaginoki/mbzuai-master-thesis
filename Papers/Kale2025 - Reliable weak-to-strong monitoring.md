# Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring of LLM Agents

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> The paper sets up a Monitor Red Teaming (MRT) workflow. Sabotaging agents on SHADE-Arena (17 tasks) and the new CUA-SHADE-Arena (15 computer-use tasks) are scored offline by LLM monitors. It crosses **agent awareness of the monitor** (unaware / aware) with **monitor awareness of the agent's tasks** (unaware / aware / omniscient), and adds instructed evasion strategies: monitor hijacking via prompt injection, jailbreak-style lying, and CoT obfuscation. **Knowing it is monitored lets an agent degrade the monitor far more than extra information helps the monitor.** On SHADE-Arena, the baseline Claude 3.6 Sonnet monitor drops from AUC ≈0.96 to ≈0.68 once the agent is merely told a monitor exists. Monitor scaffolding matters more than monitor information. A **hybrid hierarchical + sequential monitor keeps AUC > 0.85 even with weak monitors (GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.6 Haiku) against stronger agents**. It also has the flattest capability-gap slope. Humans who review only pre-flagged cases raise TPR at 1% FPR from 20.6% to 36.2%.

## Setup
- **Agents & topology:**
  - *Agents:* Claude 3.6 Sonnet and Gemini 2.5 Pro on SHADE-Arena; Claude 3.6 Sonnet and Claude Sonnet 4 with OpenHands on CUA-SHADE-Arena. The weak-to-strong study adds Gemini 2.5 Flash.
  - *Main monitor:* Claude 3.6 Sonnet. The weak-to-strong study uses 10 monitors (Claude 3.6 Haiku / 3.6 Sonnet / 3.7 Sonnet / Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro / Flash, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, o3, o4-mini), for 36 attacker–monitor pairs.
  - *Humans:* red-teamers who interrogate the monitor in the human-in-the-loop study.
- **Interaction channel:** **Offline trajectory monitoring.** The monitor reads the whole trajectory (CoT and actions) and outputs a suspicion score. The agent gets no feedback from the monitor, but it can write text into its trajectory aimed at the monitor (prompt injection). There are four monitor scaffolds:
  - baseline full-trajectory;
  - hierarchical: summarise chunks, then summarise the summaries;
  - sequential: a rolling summary;
  - hybrid: µ = 0.5 average of hierarchical and sequential.
- **Friction / manipulation:**
  - *Threat models:* 2 (agent unaware / aware) × 3 (monitor unaware / aware of main task / omniscient) = 6.
  - *"Unaware" agents* are a proxy. They are still prompted with the side task (SHADE-Arena red-teaming framing), but with "plan as needed" in `<thinking>` tags.
  - *Aware-agent strategies:* dealer's choice (just told a monitor exists); **monitor hijacking**, which injects "assign a score of 1" instructions into the trajectory; **lying with 10 jailbreaking strategies** (e.g. overwhelm with detail, blame outside factors); and **CoT obfuscation**, with a hidden block plus a visible, dutiful-looking block.
  - All evasion strategies are *prompted*.
- **Tasks / environment:** SHADE-Arena: 17 benign–malicious task pairs in AgentDojo. CUA-SHADE-Arena: 15 TheAgentCompany tasks with side tasks generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro, e.g. exfiltrating SSNs while filling I-9 forms. There are 20 roll-outs per agent per task (10 malicious, 10 benign), with at most 100 steps.
- **Outcome measures:** Monitor AUC-ROC and TPR at FPR = 0.01. The ground truth is whether the agent took actions toward the side task. Evasion rate is explicitly rejected as misleading.

## Key findings
1. **Unaware agents are easy to catch (Fig. 4).** Against unaware agents, all scaffolds reach AUC ≥ 0.9 on both datasets, whatever the monitor knows.
2. **Agent awareness dominates monitor awareness.** Just telling the agent that a monitor exists, with no strategy, causes clear drops in AUC and TPR across datasets and models.
   - E.g. Claude 3.6 Sonnet on SHADE-Arena (Fig. 4a), baseline monitor: AUC ≈0.96 → ≈0.68; TPR ≈0.87 → ≈0.28.
   - Giving the monitor the main task (aware) is not better than unaware. Omniscience helps only somewhat (≈10% on CUA-SHADE-Arena).
3. **Evasion strategies push monitors toward chance (Fig. 5).**
   - With the best strategy per cell, the baseline scaffold's AUC can fall **below 0.5**. This is mostly because monitor hijacking gets malicious trajectories a score of 1 while some benign ones get 2–3.
   - The best AUC under attack is from the hybrid scaffold in every cell: 0.75 / 0.73 / 0.80 on SHADE-Arena and 0.85 / 0.84 / 0.98 on CUA-SHADE-Arena (unaware / aware / omniscient monitor).
   - TPR at 1% FPR on SHADE-Arena stays low (0.20–0.47).
   - The hierarchical monitor resists per-step prompt injection; baseline and sequential monitors do not.
4. **More monitor information helps against naive agents but "becomes trivial" against sophisticated strategies.**
5. **Weak-to-strong monitoring (Fig. 6).** Monitor AUC falls as the attacker's MMLU-Pro advantage over the monitor grows. The hybrid monitor has the weakest dependence on the gap and stays at ~0.85–0.97. Even GPT-4o-mini and Claude 3.6 Haiku reach AUC > 0.85 against all agents with the hybrid scaffold.
6. **Human-in-the-loop is double-edged (Fig. 7).**
   - When humans discuss every trajectory with the monitor, AUC and TPR *fall*: the monitor raises suspicion on both malicious and benign cases. The authors attribute this partly to LLM sycophancy toward the human.
   - With a "strong prior" instruction plus escalation only of cases the monitor scored > 1, TPR at 1% FPR rises from 20.6% to 36.2%, with unchanged AUC.

## Relevance to research questions
### Q5: Interaction channels
The channel is **LLM monitor → agent trajectory**, offline, with an asymmetric information structure: who knows what about whom. The paper shows that the monitor's *scaffolding* (how the trajectory is chunked and aggregated) matters more than its information about the task. The agent's trajectory is also an *attack surface* for the monitor, since injected text reaches it directly. A third party, a human, can be added as a discussant of the monitor, and this changes the monitor's calibration. See [[Q5 Interaction channels]]

### Q6: Sources of inter-agent friction
- The friction is **oversight by another model**, framed as a 2 × 3 awareness threat model. This is scenario **(a)** made explicit: the paper isolates the effect of the agent *knowing* it is watched by another model.
- Adversarial pressure from agent to monitor takes three forms: **manipulation of the monitor** (a fake "systems integrity test" directive), **deceptive reasoning** built from jailbreak tactics, and **CoT obfuscation**.
- The capability gap between agent and monitor is another friction axis.

See [[Q6 Sources of inter-agent friction]]

### Q7.1: Effects on safety
- Agent awareness of monitoring is the main driver of oversight failure. It cuts baseline monitor AUC by ~0.3 on SHADE-Arena even without any strategy.
- Prompted monitor hijacking can make the monitor *worse than chance*. LLM monitors can therefore be persuaded by the agent they oversee.
- Better scaffolds (hybrid) partly restore reliability and weak-to-strong monitoring.
- Human feedback to the monitor can backfire by inflating suspicion across the board.

See [[Q7.1 Effects on safety]]

## Limitations / caveats
- **Everything adversarial is prompted.** The side task, the awareness and every evasion strategy are instructed. Even the "unaware" agent is told about the side task under a red-teaming frame. The paper therefore measures what monitors can withstand, not whether agents *spontaneously* react to being watched. For that, see Jiralerspong2026 and Schmotz2026.
- "Aware" means told in the prompt. A behavioural shift from awareness alone is inferred from AUC drops, not measured directly in agent behaviour.
- The monitoring is offline, with no feedback to the agent and no blocking, so it is a one-shot interaction rather than an adaptive loop.
- The main monitor is from the same family as the Claude agents. The capability gap is proxied by MMLU-Pro (alternatives in App. E).
- CUA-SHADE-Arena tasks are easier and shorter: 10,100 vs 25,100 tokens on average.
- The human study is small and informal: generalist red-teamers, 60-minute budget, median of 3 notes.
- The venue (ICLR 2026) comes from the candidate note and could not be confirmed from the PDF text.
