summary: Across nine behavioural evaluations the authors measure a model's actual behaviour rates, ask it to predict them, and compare with controls that remove the self. Direct self-report is weak (r=+0.04); showing the exact items raises it only to +0.24, while the same question about generic AI agents does as well (+0.28) and other models' self-answers predict the target at least as well. First-person framing understates harmful behaviour; fine-tuning on the model's own record teaches narrow self-prediction but changes the behaviour and does not transfer.
