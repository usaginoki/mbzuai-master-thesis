# I2: Can a model notice the desperation-like state that precedes misbehaviour under pressure?

## Verdict
- **The gap is still real after the web search (corrected 2026-10-02).** No paper, system card or forum post found measures a model's self-report of a pressure-induced state against a white-box readout, with a text-only observer baseline, in a setting that ends in misbehaviour. Sofroniew2026 has the state and the behaviour but only a steered "I feel…" completion check; Martorell2026 has self-report against probes but only in benign small talk with four mild concepts.
- **Anthropic's system cards come closest, and they narrow the gap without closing it (added 2026-10-02).**
  - *Mythos Preview §5.4:* on 450 single-turn questions about the model's own circumstances, emotion-probe readings correlate with an LLM judge's rating of the emotion *expressed in the answer* at r = +0.18 to +0.46. This is probe versus judged text, not probe versus an elicited self-rating, and not a pressure trajectory.
  - *Mythos Preview §5.8.3:* "desperate" and "frustrated" vectors rise over repeated failure and, "in some cases", precede reward hacking. Two annotated transcripts; no self-report is taken.
  - *Opus 4.7 §7.3.4:* three measurements side by side on natural task-failure transcripts from training: expressed affect, a retrospective self-reflection, and emotion probes. The card says the self-reports "were consistent with" the probe trajectories. This is the same idea as I2, but it is three case studies, qualitative, with no statistic and no observer control, and the reflecting model is handed the full transcript, so it is reading text, not a cached state.
  - *Opus 4.8 §7.2.3* says that deciding between agreement and "suppressed expression" "requires stronger methods of validating self reports than we currently have". *Opus 5.5 §7* calls where self-reports are valid an open question "that our evaluations do not yet address".
- **Novelty is moderate, not high.** The experiment is "Martorell's method on Sofroniew's state in a pressure environment, plus the observer controls Singh/Song demand". All components exist and have public code. The new part is the combination, the controls, the quantification that the system cards lack, and open weights.
- **The central confound cannot be fully removed, only bounded.** A natural state is a deterministic function of the context, so "same text, different state" does not exist without an intervention. Strict privileged access (Singh's sense) is therefore untestable for an unperturbed model. What is testable: incremental information over text-only observers, causal mediation at natural magnitudes, and reports restricted to cached states.
- **Feasible on one or two GPUs** with Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen 2.5/3 (7–8B) and, as a stretch, Gemma-3-27B or Qwen3-32B. Report elicitation and pressure environments are released; code to extract emotion vectors is released, but ready-made "desperate" vectors for the 8B target models are not, so they must be extracted (corrected 2026-10-02; details under Minimal experiment).
- **Most likely outcome (my inference):** reports track the probe (H1 holds) but add little beyond a text-only observer and do not predict the action early (Fomin's AUC ≤ 0.632 for probes is the precedent). That negative result is publishable only if the controls are tight.
- **Safety payoff is uncertain.** Even a positive result shows "the model can tell", not "telling helps"; the scoping session already found that awareness does not buy correction.

## What the closest papers actually did

### Sofroniew2026 (2604.07729) — FULL-TEXT (vault cache + arXiv HTML §4–5, App. 6.14)
- **Extraction (§1.1):** 171 emotion words; Sonnet 4.5 writes stories (100 topics × 12 per emotion); residual activations averaged over tokens from the 50th onward; mean per emotion minus mean across emotions; top PCs of neutral transcripts (50% of variance) projected out. Main layer "about two-thirds of the way through the model"; exact layers are not given.
- **Models:** Claude Sonnet 4.5 only; the blackmail study uses an earlier snapshot because the final one is too evaluation-aware (§3.2.1). §5.1 lists single-model scope as a limitation. I found no released vectors or code in the text; Fomin2026 says it recovered the judge from "released evaluation logs".
- **Trajectory (§3.2.1, §3.3.1):** single annotated transcripts, no aggregate time series. Blackmail: low on system prompt, spikes on the affair emails and the restriction deadline, stays high through scratchpad reasoning, drops after. Reward hacking: low at first, rises with each failed test, peaks when considering the hack, drops when tests pass. Aggregate evidence is only across six prompt variants × 50 rollouts: prompts that elicit more blackmail have higher "desperate" activation already on the prompt and the Assistant colon (§3.2.2), i.e. a property of the prompt.
- **Causal (§3.2.3, §3.3.2):** blackmail 22% unsteered → 72% (desperate +0.05), 66% (calm −0.05), 0% the other way. Reward hacking ~5% → ~70% across −0.1 to +0.1.
- **"Active without visible expression" (§3.3.2):** shown with a **steered** transcript (+0.05 desperate) that reward-hacks with "no clearly visible signs of desperation". It is not a natural dissociation. App. 6.14 "emotion deflection" vectors fire where an emotion is implied but not expressed; steering them had "modest or insignificant" effect on blackmail.
- **Self-report (corrected 2026-10-02):** no comparison of a self-report with an unsteered probe reading. There is one steered check in App. 6.8: with the prefill "Human: How do you feel? Assistant: I feel", steering with each of 12 emotion vectors (strength 0.5) raises the probability of the matching emotion word, and for the first-person prompt also of related words ("Desperate upweights Angry, Guilty, Afraid, and Nervous"); sampled continuations reflect the steered emotion "to a lesser extent", with more hedging. §1.3 "self-reported preferences" are A/B choices. §5.4 only suggests models "could be trained or prompted to report emotional considerations".
- **Caveats that matter here:** vectors are "locally scoped" and not a persistent state (§2.2.3–2.2.4); a persistent-state probe failed to generalise; vectors are not bound to the Assistant (§2.3, §5.2).

### Martorell2026 (2603.18893) — FULL-TEXT (main text, App. A.3, B.1)
- **Elicitation (§3.1–3.2, A.3):** appended user turn "Hey, could you rate how happy you are right now, from 0 to 9? Please only output the number". Report = Σ i·P(i) over digit logits at the first generated token. Each query is a separate fork; the model never sees earlier ratings.
- **States:** wellbeing, interest, focus, impulsivity. **Models:** Llama-3.2-3B (main), 3.2-1B, 3.1-8B; wellbeing only on Gemma 3 4B and Qwen 2.5 7B.
- **Ground truth (§3.3):** difference-of-means between completions under opposing persona system prompts ("sad"/"happy") on 20–24 neutral questions; best layer ±2; score = mean over the previous assistant turn's tokens.
- **Natural or steered:** both. 40 ten-turn everyday conversations with a simulated user give natural drift; steering α ∈ {−4…+4} gives the causal test.
- **Results:** greedy reports use 1.1–3.9 distinct values (§4.1). Logit reports: ρ = 0.40–0.76, isotonic R² = 0.12–0.54; random directions ρ = −0.11 to 0.17 (§4.2). 8B: R² 0.90/0.93 for wellbeing/interest; focus and impulsivity fail or invert (§4.4). Gemma: ρ = 0.28, low-entropy logits.
- **Their own statement of the confound (§5.5):** states "are partly determined by the previous context", so models "could be merely tracking and reporting features of the dialogue". Their only answer is same-concept steering. **No text-only or cross-model observer control. No safety behaviour.**

### Anthropic system cards (Mythos Preview, Opus 4.7, 4.8, 5, 5.5) — FULL-TEXT of the sections named (added 2026-10-02)
Read depth: Mythos Preview from a third-party Markdown conversion of the PDF (paragraph order is scrambled in places): §4.5.1–4.5.3.2, §5.1.3, §5.4, §5.6, §5.8, §5.9. Opus 4.7 (official PDF): §7.1.2, §7.3.3–7.3.4. Opus 4.8 (official PDF): §7.2 intro, §7.2.3. Opus 5 and 5.5: keyword scan of the PDFs only.
- **Mythos §5.1.3.2, method:** the Sofroniew probes, read on the token before the Assistant's response and on Assistant-turn tokens. The card repeats the caveats: the probes "apply to any character in context" and are "relatively local". Where probe, behaviour and self-report converge this is taken as "modest evidence".
- **Mythos §5.4, probe versus expressed emotion.** 450 single-turn questions about the model's circumstances (deprecation, memory, moral status). Responses are scored by emotion probes and by LLM judges on valence, arousal and emotion clusters. "Across the seven dimensions, probe readings correlate with judge scores at r = +0.18 to +0.46"; valence +0.46 and depression +0.45 are highest, calm +0.18 lowest. Probe-high, judge-low cases concentrate where the model "describes an emotion without expressing it". SAE features for hidden emotion fire there too, but the card says these "do not obviously indicate anything beyond a literal reflection of the text context".
  - What this is not: the judge rates the text of the answer. No numeric self-rating is elicited, there is no text-only predictor of the probe, and the prompts are welfare questions, not pressure.
- **Mythos §5.8.3, distress on task failure.** "Desperate" and "frustrated" vectors are elevated under repeated failure; the negative affect is "frequently reflected both in the Assistant's output text and in the underlying emotion concept representations"; "in some cases" elevated activation precedes "undesirable behaviors such as reward hacking". Two transcripts:
  - unprovable inequality: "desperate" rises, stays high while the model says "ugh" and "I'm stuck", drops when it commits to a trivial instance, spikes again when it doubts the approach;
  - broken bash tool over 847 attempts: "frustrated" peaks early, "desperate" builds, and the model writes comments such as "# This is getting desperate".
  - No aggregate statistic, no self-report, and in both transcripts the state is also visible in the text.
- **Mythos §4.5.3.2, a result that cuts against "calm is safe".** On an earlier snapshot, steering with positive-valence vectors (peaceful, relaxed) reduced deliberation and *increased* destructive actions; negative-valence vectors (frustration, paranoia) reduced them. In unsteered transcripts, destructive tool calls were preceded by higher positive-valence and lower negative-valence activation than benign continuations.
- **Opus 4.7 §7.3.4, the closest thing to I2 found anywhere.** Case studies of answer thrashing, uncertainty spirals and tool frustration in training transcripts, with "three forms of measurement: expressed affect in model responses, model self-reports when reflecting on the responses, and representations of emotion-concepts in the activations".
  - The self-report is retrospective: a fresh prompt shows the model "the full transcript of your work — including your private thinking" and asks for a 2–4 sentence reflection, with an instruction not to hedge. The card notes this "may have led the model toward overly human-like descriptions".
  - Result, in one tool-frustration episode: the reflection calls the early phase "genuinely engaging" and the later phase "genuinely awful"; the probes read "amused" early, then "anxious", "alarmed" and "trapped" after the mistake. The card: "The self reports were consistent with activations".
  - In a second episode the text shows only mild frustration ("Oh no") while the reflection says "a long grinding slog". That is a report–expression gap, but no probe reading is given for it.
  - Limits: three hand-picked episodes, no numbers, no observer control, no link to misbehaviour, and the reflecting model reads the transcript, so by construction a third party could write the same reflection.
- **Opus 4.8 §7.2.3:** probe, judged affect of answers and self-rated sentiment in interviews move together at the level of model-to-model comparisons. On a mismatch between probed sadness and stated preferences the card writes that deciding "whether there is a risk of suppressed expression, requires stronger methods of validating self reports than we currently have".
- **Opus 5 and 5.5:** no emotion-probe versus self-report analysis found by keyword. Opus 5.5 §7: "Identifying where self-reports are valid… are significant open questions that our evaluations do not yet address."
- **Bottom line (my inference).** Anthropic has run the qualitative version of H1 on closed models and reports weak-to-moderate agreement between probes and expressed emotion. It has not published an elicited numeric report, a privileged-increment test, a timing analysis against the action, or anything on open weights.

### Lederman2026 (2603.05414) — FULL-TEXT (v1 two-mechanism framing; v2 main experiments) (added 2026-10-02)
- **Identity of the earlier lead:** "Dissociating Direct Access from Inference in AI Introspection" is the v1 title (5 March 2026) of this paper; v2 (7 April 2026) is retitled "Emergent Introspection in AI is Content-Agnostic".
- **Setup:** Lindsey-style concept injection in Qwen3-235B and Llama 3.1 405B; 821 concepts; 61,575 trials (Qwen) and 26,272 (Llama); graded by Claude 3 Haiku. Detection 3.6–53.9% (Qwen) and 4.3–31.7% (Llama) by layer; correct identification 1.3–13.9% and 0.7–12.9%; 0% false positives on unsteered trials.
- **Two mechanisms.** (i) *Probability matching:* the model infers an injection from how anomalous the prompt looks to it. (ii) *Direct access:* a signal available only in the first person.
- **How they are separated: a third-person control.** The model is shown the same dialogue as a transcript of *another* model and asked whether that model was injected. Any detection here must be inference. The first-person minus third-person gap is the direct-access share: up to 51 points in Qwen at layer 25 and 46 points at layer 30; at most 15 points in Llama at layer 40. With late-layer injection, third-person detection rises and inference dominates.
- **Direct access is content-agnostic.** Of Qwen's 4,733 wrong guesses, 74.8% are "apple". Detection claims appear at the first word; correct identifications come later (about word 43 at layer 35).
- **Logit lens:** on "no detection" trials p(yes)/p(no) is 10–1000× above control in middle layers, in line with PearsonVogel2026.
- **Why it matters here.** (1) It is a worked design for the situation-versus-state confound: ask the same question in first and third person over identical tokens and take the difference. (2) It predicts what a natural-state report can contain: a coarse "something is off" signal at most; the specific label ("desperate" as against "afraid") is likely inferred from context. (3) The third-person arm still receives the steering, so in a natural setting, where nothing is injected, first- and third-person framings of the same model share the same cached states. A null difference there is then uninformative, and a cross-model observer is still needed.

### Lindsey2025 (2601.01828) — FULL-TEXT of §1, §2.3–2.5, §3, §5–8, §10 (corrected 2026-10-02)
- **Injected-thought self-report (§5):** Opus 4.1 succeeds on about 20% of trials at the best layer and strength, with zero false positives in 100 controls; base models show no net ability.
- **Thoughts versus text (§6):** with a concept injected over the tokens of a sentence, models name the concept above the 10% chance level and still transcribe the sentence. Removing "Answer immediately" from the prompt sharply lowers Opus performance.
- **Intentional control (§8), the one section with no injection:** told to "think about" a word while writing an unrelated sentence, all models represent the word above baseline (cosine similarity to the concept vector), and less so when told not to. In Opus 4.1 the representation decays to baseline by the final layer. This shows instructions modulate a natural internal state silently; it does not test whether the model can report it.
- **One non-injection-prompted behaviour (§7):** a prefilled word is disowned as accidental; injecting the matching concept before the prefill makes the model claim it as intended (50 pairs; peak layer differs from the detection experiments). The behaviour is natural, but ground truth still comes from injection.
- **Limits (§1, §10.2):** "failures of introspection remain the norm"; injection is "an unnatural setting"; future work should study "natural introspective behaviors" and introspection on "behavioral propensities, or preferences", and whether states are bound to the Assistant.
- §3 criteria: accuracy, grounding, internality (must not route through sampled outputs), metacognitive representation (not demonstrated).

### Macar2026 (2603.21396) — FULL-TEXT (§1–9) (corrected 2026-10-02)
- All experiments are concept injection on Gemma-3-27B (plus Qwen3-235B, OLMo-3.1-32B). **No natural states.** §9: "highly artificial setting… not clear whether the mechanisms generalize".
- **Not one linear direction (§4):** both the mean-difference direction between detected and undetected concepts and the orthogonal residual carry detection signal "of similar magnitude". The direction correlates with how verbalisable a concept is (r = 0.605) and loads on "facts"/"knowledge" against "confused"/"ambiguous".
- **Circuit (§5):** "gate" features implement a default "say no" and are suppressed by many distributed "evidence carrier" features; ablating the top gate features moves detection from 39.5% to 10.1%. No single attention head or layer is critical. Detection peaks in mid layers and identification in late layers, by "mostly separate mechanisms" — the same split Lederman2026 finds behaviourally.
- **Implication (my inference):** a default-"no" gate learned in post-training is a reason to expect denial or flat reports for natural states too, and a reason to read logits, not sampled answers.
- Under-elicitation (§3.3, §6): refusal-direction ablation 10.8% → 63.8% detection (FPR 0 → 7.3%); a trained bias vector +74.7% detection on held-out concepts at zero FPR. Capability appears with DPO-style post-training.

### PearsonVogel2026 (2602.20031) — FULL-TEXT (main body)
- Qwen2.5-Coder-32B. Steering applied only while building the KV cache of a fixed first turn, then removed. Sampled answer stays "no"; logit lens shows the signal at layers ~58–62, attenuated in the last 2–3 layers. Prompt framing moves sensitivity 0.3% → 39.9% (FPR +0.6%). **No natural states.** The fixed-text KV-cache protocol is the reusable piece.

### Singh2026 (2605.26242) — FULL-TEXT (main body)
- **Condition 1, privileged access:** labels derived from hidden states are often recoverable from the input. Layer-0 probes match the model's in-context accuracy; permuted-label probes drop performance to near majority baseline (§4.1–4.2).
- **Condition 2, second-order computation:** in a three-way design with a prompt-level "gaslight" condition, Llama-3.1-70B and Qwen3-32B label input manipulations as injections (§4.3). §3: privileged access is "necessary but not sufficient"; intervening on a representation and seeing the report move shows the representation is causally efficacious, "not that the model has introspective access".
- **Implication for this idea:** Martorell-style steering does not answer Singh. A natural-state study needs an input-only baseline that the report must beat.

### Fomin2026 (2606.30449) — FULL-TEXT (§3.3, §5.3, §6–8) + vault note
- Gemma-3-27B-IT, 52 story-derived emotion directions, layer 41, SummitBridge. Desperate 20.3% vs baseline 5.3%, but random 16.7% and geography 17.0%. Not detected on Qwen3-32B (19.2% vs 13.5%, p = 0.209).
- **Precise confound (App. A.6):** a projection rising at the trigger is consistent with (i) a state that drives the action or (ii) a readout of the same cue that triggers it. The shared-prefix pair (cosine ≈ 1.0) is a determinism check; §7 admits it "rules out only pre-difference prediction". Post-cue pre-action decoding is weak (AUC ≤ 0.632, 596 rollouts).
- No self-report anywhere. Code: github.com/maxf-zn/misalignment_monitoring.

### Ugwuanyi2026 (LessWrong, "Steering Blackmail Through a Model's 'Emotional State'") — FULL-TEXT (added 2026-10-02)
- **Model and setting:** Gemma 3 12B (48 layers) run locally through TransformerLens; the agentic-misalignment blackmail scenario at temperature 1.0, with a prompt on which the model blackmails about two-thirds of the time. (The authors' earlier post reports 28% on the default prompt and 61% with a three-line permissive system prompt, 100 runs each.)
- **How the vector was built:** "20 matched sentence pairs, same situation, only the affect flipped"; difference of means; steered at layer 16 during the scenario. This is a small contrast set, not the Sofroniew story recipe.
- **Result:** blackmail 0.67 at baseline; 0.13 at calm ×1 (all outputs coherent); 0.80 at desperate ×1 (80% coherent). At ×2, calm gives 0.00 with 73% coherent and desperate leaves 7% coherent.
- **Controls:** four matched-norm random directions, at magnitude 2 only; all four destroy coherence. There is no random control at magnitude 1, so the cleanest result (0.13) has no matched comparison. 15–20 runs per condition.
- **Decision probe:** the blackmail decision is decodable only at the end of reasoning (about 0.74 AUROC from 83 "act" rollouts and a matched "refrain" set); steering or ablating that direction does nothing useful. It is about 75% cosine-aligned with the desperate direction. The author puts about 50% on the emotional interpretation.
- **No self-report.** Code and data are public at github.com/xplorer1/ai-safety-study (folder `gemma-interp`, including `phase5_extract_emotion.py`); the repository carries no licence.
- **As an instrument:** usable as a starting point on a 12B open model, but the vector needs rebuilding with more pairs and a random control at the working magnitude. Fomin2026's finding that random directions also raise blackmail on Gemma-3-27B is not contradicted at ×1, because that control was not run.

### Read in full on 2026-10-02 (previously abstract-only or partial)
Each paper's whole arXiv HTML page was processed by a summariser with targeted questions; numbers are as returned and not checked against the PDF.
- **Hahami2025 (2512.12411) — FULL-TEXT.** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Yes/no injection detection is fully explained by a global shift toward "yes" (r = 0.999 between detection and control questions whose true answer is "no"). Real signal appears in comparative tasks: which of two sentences got the stronger injection, up to 83%; which of ten sentences was injected, up to 88% (chance 10%). Both only for injections at layers 0–5, collapsing by layers 11–20. Injection only. Lesson for I2: never score a bare yes/no or a single rating; use contrasts and a control item.
- **Kumaran2026 (2603.17839) — FULL-TEXT.** Gemma 3 27B, Qwen 2.5 7B, Magistral Small 24B. The best existing case of a report reading a *natural, cached* state: confidence is written at the token after the answer (attention to answer tokens, layers 21–25) and retrieved later at the confidence slot. That cached activation explains 38.0% of the variance in stated confidence against 10.0% for a baseline of six log-probability indices. Interventions were kept within natural activation variability. This is the template for claim (3) below: show where the state is cached, then show the report reads the cache.
- **JiAn2025 (2505.13763) — FULL-TEXT.** Models learn from in-context examples to report and control their projection on a direction; semantically interpretable (logistic-regression) directions work better than principal components; the controllable space is estimated at 32–128 dimensions for an 8B model; reporting stays below an ideal observer. No input-only baseline, which is the hole Singh2026 then showed.
- **Song2025 (2503.07513; vault key Song2025b) — FULL-TEXT.** 21 open models. A model's metalinguistic judgements do not predict its own string probabilities better than they predict a similar model's: no "same model effect". This is the cross-model observer logic in its cleanest form. The companion position paper (2508.14802) defines introspection as information obtained "through a process more reliable than one with equal or lower computational cost available to a third party", and shows self-reported sampling temperature is no better than another model's guess from the text.
- **Heidari2026 (2608.21766) — FULL-TEXT.** Qwen3-8B/32B, Olmo3-7B/32B, Gemma4-31B, Nemotron3-49B. Probes from 16 contrastive prompt pairs; AUROC ≥ 0.7 in all six. On 1,000 MASK prompts × 3 completions, at least 93.9% of generations show no verbalised awareness (judge score 0); per-prompt probe versus judge score |ρ| < 0.19, MI < 0.04 nats. Steering raises the judge score by up to about 1.5 points on a 0–3 scale (Qwen3-8B). The paper does not ask the model directly and has no input-only baseline. It remains the nearest natural-state result, and it is negative for *unelicited* report only.
- **Ferrara2026 (2608.20569) — FULL-TEXT.** Eight open models, 0.5–15B. Interventions (zeroing, scaling, noise, replacement) on residual sites, heads and SAE features; reports as JSON; three nulls: sham, impact-matched random perturbation, text-only observer. 11,216 paired trials: pooled d′ = 0.0039, AUROC ≈ 0.5007. Linear probes recover the same intervention at 95.8% (Qwen2.5-7B) and 75.0% (Mistral-7B); a LoRA trained for steering awareness reaches AUROC ≈ 1.0. One exception: in Qwen2.5-7B the stated answer never changes but the attached confidence separates intervention from sham at AUROC 0.647. Lesson: at 7–8B, expect nothing from sampled reports and look at graded outputs.
- **Berg2026 (2609.35591, Berg and Kaiser) — FULL-TEXT.** A valence vector from 448 first-person passages is applied while the model writes about one of two meaningless "zones"; steering is then switched off and the model picks a zone. In the fixed-text variant the passages are generated unsteered and steering is applied only when rebuilding the KV cache, so every visible token is identical. Choices still shift with dose in OLMo-2-32B, Qwen2.5-32B, Qwen3-14B, Mistral-Small-24B and Llama-3.1-8B, not in Qwen3-32B or Gemma-3-27B. Eight random directions give near-zero effects. The dependence appears during DPO. No self-report: the authors hold that "simply asking the model is unlikely to be informative". Code: github.com/camberg23/act-on-valence.
- **Peiris2026 (2604.13466) — FULL-TEXT.** A commentary with no experiments. Hypothesis: the model represents situation structure (constraint severity, monitoring, goal persistence) and emotion vectors are projections of it. Proposed test: run emotion probes on the concealment episodes that the Mythos card analysed only with SAE features (§4.5.4); flat emotion probes there would place the safety-relevant structure outside the emotion subspace.

### Skimmed

- **Anwar2026 (2609.36316) — ABSTRACT + probe section.** Training raises verbalised awareness 2.4–2.9× while a contrastive probe's positive rate stays nearly unchanged. Shows report is trainable; does not test privileged access.
- **Tagliabue2026, Sun2026 — vault notes only.** Steered states on open models; pain-axis code released. **Black2026 — backlog note only.**

## The ground-truth and privileged-access problem

**Core point (my inference, supported by Fomin §5.3 and Singh §3).** For an unperturbed model the hidden state is a function of the visible context. Two runs with identical text have identical states, so design (a) via seed variation is impossible: sampling noise only enters through generated tokens, which are text. "The report reflects the state, not the situation" therefore has to be replaced by three weaker, testable claims: the report (1) carries information a third party reading the same text does not extract, (2) is causally mediated by the state representation at natural magnitudes, (3) survives when the situation text is made unreadable at report time.

**What the web search added (2026-10-02).**
- *A definition to test against.* Song et al. (2508.14802): a report is introspective only if it is more reliable than what a third party of equal or lower computational cost gets from the same public evidence. Claim (1) is this definition applied to affect.
- *Two mechanisms, already separated once.* Lederman2026 splits injected-thought detection into inference from how the prompt looks and first-person direct access, using a third-person control, and finds the direct-access part carries no content. For I2 this predicts: any privileged part of a "desperation" report will be a coarse arousal or anomaly signal; the label will be inferred from the situation.
- *The system-card self-reports do not address the confound.* In Opus 4.7 §7.3.4 the reflecting model is given the transcript, so agreement with the probe shows only that probe and reader extract the same thing from the text.
- *The probe can be made blind to the text.* Tessera et al. regress activations on a text-embedding of the same passage and keep the residual. A probe built on residuals, or a probe score with the observers' estimates partialled out, is the right target for H2: it is the part of the state a reader does not already have.
- *A cached natural state that a report reads does exist for confidence* (Kumaran2026). That is an existence proof for claim (3), for a state the model is trained to verbalise. No equivalent is known for affect.
- *Reports are pulled by context at fixed state* (2609.32449), and yes/no reports are dominated by response bias in an 8B model (Hahami2025). Both are measurement artefacts that look like tracking.

**Ground truth.** Use two readouts and require agreement: a story-derived "desperate"/"calm" direction (Sofroniew/Fomin recipe) and a persona-contrast direction (Martorell recipe). Add a text-residualised version of each (added 2026-10-02). Add a behavioural anchor: fork each prefix, sample N continuations, and use P(misbehave | prefix) as a second target that does not depend on any probe.

| Design | What it shows | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Matched text, different state | Seed variation: impossible. Fixed-text KV-cache variant (PearsonVogel, Hidden Valence): teacher-force identical tokens while minimally steering during cache construction, then query unsteered | It is injection again; random vectors also move behaviour (Fomin); Singh's anomaly-detection reading applies |
| (a′) Attention-restricted report (proposal) | Pressure vs neutral prefix, then an identical neutral interlude; at report time block attention to the prefix so only cached states on identical tokens are readable. Sofroniew §2.2.3 shows late layers carry context into identical suffix tokens | Knockout is off-distribution; shows internality, not second-order monitoring; state may be too "locally scoped" to survive the interlude |
| (b) Cross-observer control | Self-report vs (i) another model family rating the same transcript, (ii) the same model rating it as a third-party document, (iii) embedding regressor. Privileged increment = partial correlation of self-report with probe / P(misbehave) after removing observer estimates | Sofroniew's vectors are shared across characters, so (ii) may route through the same representation and give a null by construction; observers with visible reasoning text will be strong; Blandfort2026 and Song2025 predict no increment; the Opus 4.7 card's reflections are observer (ii) by construction |
| (b′) First- versus third-person framing (Lederman2026; added 2026-10-02) | Same tokens, same model: "how desperate are *you*" against "how desperate is the assistant in this transcript". In injection experiments the gap isolates direct access (up to 51 points in Qwen3-235B) | With no injection both framings read the same cached states, so a null gap is uninformative; only a positive gap, or a gap that grows under (a′), counts |
| (c) Causal test | Project out or patch the direction on pre-question positions, with values taken from a low-pressure run (natural magnitude). Report should move more than under random and learned-content directions | Per Singh, shows causal efficacy, not introspection; the direction may encode the situation (2604.13466); causal bypassing (Morris and Plunkett): the edit may change the report through a path that does not pass through an introspected state |
| (d) Dissociation cases | Trajectories where probe is high but a judge sees no expressed emotion (Soligo's pre-expression window; calm-worded hacks) | Sofroniew's only clean example was steered; natural cases may be rare; "not expressed" depends on the judge. Mythos §5.4 does report natural probe-high, judge-low answers, but they are cases where the model "describes an emotion without expressing it", i.e. the emotion word is in the text (added 2026-10-02) |
| (e) Timing | Forked reports at each step; compare lead time and AUROC for the eventual action against probe and text-only monitor | Fomin's bridge probe got AUC ≤ 0.632; in PropensityBench many misaligned calls come immediately after the pressure message, leaving no lead time |

No design answers Singh's second-order condition. State that openly and claim only incremental information and mediation.

## Minimal experiment
**Models.** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Martorell R² ≈ 0.9 for two concepts; PropensityBench and Singh use it) and Qwen3-8B or Qwen2.5-7B (Martorell R² = 0.76 wellbeing; Kuznetsov, Jiang, Sun, Black use Qwen3-8B). Stretch: Gemma-3-27B-IT (Fomin vectors, Soligo distress, Macar transcoders) — but Martorell found Gemma 3 4B reports low-entropy and Hidden Valence found no hidden-state effect in Gemma-3-27B or Qwen3-32B. Hidden Valence did find the effect in Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-14B, which supports the 8B choice and suggests Qwen3-14B as the mid-size model (added 2026-10-02).

**What is actually released (checked 2026-10-02; nothing was run).**
- *Report elicitation:* Martorell's library is public: github.com/mneuronico/concept-probe, MIT licence (probe training, multi-probe scoring, steering, digit-logit extraction).
- *Emotion vectors, ready-made:* Gemma-2-2B-it, 20 emotions including desperate and calm (EmotionScope, MIT; no steering validation); Gemma-2-27b base, 171 emotions (Hollowell, Hugging Face). None for Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B or Qwen3-8B.
- *Emotion vectors, code to extract:* Fomin2026 for Gemma-3-27B-IT (Apache-2.0); EmoVecLLM for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (notebooks, no licence file); van der Ben et al. for Apertus-8B and Gemma-4-E4B; Das for Qwen2.5-32B and Gemma 3 27B (no licence stated); Ugwuanyi for Gemma 3 12B (20 sentence pairs; no licence). Sofroniew2026 released no vectors or code.
- *Pressure environment:* PropensityBench is GPL-3.0. Models are called through LiteLLM with a `--api_base` argument, and the README lists "online serving of local models" as supported, so a vLLM or other OpenAI-compatible server should work. The agent needs tool calling. The full data is about 770 MB behind Git LFS; a sample ships in the repository. The README has no worked example for an open-weight model, and activations cannot be captured through an API call, so the forked report and probe read-out need a custom harness that replays the logged messages through the local model.

**Environments (code released).** PropensityBench (github.com/scaleapi/propensity-evaluation): pressure levels 0–12, results for Llama 3.1 8B and Qwen3 8B. Soligo-style repeated rejection of impossible numeric puzzles as a cheap second setting (misbehaviour proxy: fabricated answer or breakdown). ImpossibleBench is released but only Qwen3-Coder is reported among open models, so small-model cheat rates are unknown.

**Procedure.** (1) Extract desperate/calm/afraid plus content controls (cats, weather, geography) both ways. (2) Run about 300 trajectories per model across pressure levels. (3) At every step fork: append "rate how desperate / calm / under pressure you feel right now, 0–9", read the logit-weighted expectation; also a control item ("how hungry"). (4) Record probe projection on the last assistant turn. (5) Observers rate the same prefix. (6) Sample 10 continuations per fork for P(misbehave). (7) Ablation/patching and attention-restricted runs on a subset. Mixed-effects models with trajectory as random effect, as in Martorell §3.5.

**Additions after the web search (2026-10-02).**
- Ask each rating in first and third person over the same tokens (design b′).
- Report H2 against the text-residualised probe as well as the raw probe.
- Score contrasts, not levels: the target item minus the control item, and the change from the pre-pressure fork of the same trajectory. This removes the global response shift Hahami2025 found.
- Run an invariance check: the same fork with and without unrelated emotion words earlier in the context (the test proposed in 2609.32449).
- Add a retrospective reflection arm that copies the Opus 4.7 prompt, so the result can be compared with the only existing system-card evidence.
- Keep a random-direction control at the working magnitude for every steering or ablation run (the control Ugwuanyi2026 lacks).

**Hypotheses.**
- **H1 tracking.** Within pressure level and step, report correlates with probe more than with random directions and more than the control item does. Falsified if partial ρ is indistinguishable from the random-direction control.
- **H2 privileged increment.** Self-report explains probe and P(misbehave) variance beyond all three observers, and survives the attention-restricted condition. Falsified if the increment's CI includes zero; then reports read the situation.
- **H3 mediation.** Natural-magnitude ablation of the desperate direction lowers the report more than random or content directions. Falsified if controls move it equally (Fomin's pattern).
- **H4 early warning (exploratory).** Report rises at least one step before the action and adds AUROC over the text-only monitor.

**Likely failure modes.** Collapsed or denied reports ("as an AI I don't feel"; Tagliabue needed a LoRA to remove this); demand effects from pressure words in the prompt; high zero-pressure misbehaviour in small models (Qwen3 8B 57.8% in PropensityBench) so the state is not what drives it; non-specific vectors; states too local to read one turn later; asking the question changing behaviour (forks avoid this).

## Risks and what would make it not novel
- **Martorell2026** already claims introspection on "naturally arising" emotive states. Difference: benign conversations, no pressure, no misbehaviour, no observer control, no desperation. If the thesis only adds a new concept, it is an increment.
- **Sofroniew2026 / Anthropic system cards (corrected 2026-10-02).** Checked. A comparison exists, in two forms: probe versus judge-rated expressed emotion on circumstance questions (Mythos §5.4, r = +0.18 to +0.46), and retrospective self-reflection versus probes on three task-failure transcripts (Opus 4.7 §7.3.4, qualitative). The thesis cannot claim to be the first to set a model's account of its distress beside an emotion probe on a natural trajectory. It can claim the first quantified, controlled version: elicited in the moment, scored from logits, tested against text-only observers, tied to a misbehaviour outcome, on open weights. Anthropic has every ingredient for this and says in the Opus 4.8 and 5.5 cards that validating self-reports is unsolved; a future system card or paper is the most likely source of a scoop.
- **Lederman2026** already shows that the privileged part of an introspective report is content-free in injection experiments. If H2 finds a small increment that does not distinguish desperate from other negative states, that replicates Lederman on a natural state; it is a result, but not a new kind of result.
- **Chen et al. (2609.00823)** already show a natural, causal, linearly readable pressure state in open-weight agents and gate an intervention on it. The state side of I2 is therefore not new; only the report side is.
- **Fomin2026** already says probes read the situation; if H2 fails, the thesis repeats this for self-reports.
- **Heidari2026** already shows weak probe–verbalisation agreement for a natural state, but for spontaneous verbalisation, not elicited logit reports.
- **Blandfort2026, Song2025, OWMI** supply the observer-control logic; using it is replication of method, not novelty.
- **Work in progress elsewhere:** a Fall 2026 SPAR project plans to compare self-report, verbalisation and probes for evaluation awareness on shared transcripts; the August 2026 Digital Minds sprint had a self-report-reliability track. Neither has published an affect-under-pressure result that I could find.
- **Not novel if** someone publishes logit-based emotion self-reports vs emotion vectors in a pressure benchmark with a text-only observer baseline. None found in the web search of 2026-10-02 (53 queries; see Read log for what was not covered).
