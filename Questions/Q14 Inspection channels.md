# Q14: Through which channels do LLM agents inspect or monitor other agents?

> [!warning] Evidence level
> Drafted on 2026-10-02 from a search plus the vault's processed multi-agent papers. Numbers for processed papers come from their notes; numbers for candidates come from abstracts or a summarised full-text fetch. No new paper was processed for this answer, so check figures against the paper before quoting.

> [!summary] Short answer
> - **Four families of channel.** (1) What the agent *emits*: final messages, tool-call logs, code diffs and shared artefacts, and its reasoning trace. (2) What the overseer *asks for*: status reports, self-report tools, questionnaires, interrogation. (3) What the overseer *provokes*: resampling, honeypots, test inputs. (4) What the overseer *reads off the substrate*: activations (probes, SAE features, activation oracles, natural-language autoencoders), telemetry and identity metadata.
> - **Text channels are informative but owned by the monitored agent.** Monitors catch unaware agents at AUC ≥ 0.9, then lose most of that once the agent knows (≈0.96 → ≈0.68) or tunes its attack (94.9% → 60.3% catch rate). Channels that let the agent *talk* to the overseer (interrogation, PR narratives, confident closing messages) make things worse, not better.
> - **More access is not monotonically better.** CoT + action monitors are usually beaten by filtered views (+16.8 pp), agent auditors with white-box tools are beaten by scaffolded black-box tools, and LLM judges are beaten by a TF-IDF detector on false success (AUROC ≤ 0.65 vs 0.83–0.95).
> - **Activations are the only channel shown to survive the agent knowing about it,** in one small study: collusion probes stay at 0.93–0.99 AUROC when agents are told they are probed. But the reader there is a classifier, not an agent; LLM agents that wield white-box tools exist only for offline audits (13% → 42% on a hidden-goal game).
> - **"Health" readouts of another agent barely exist.** The nearest are a questionnaire-plus-Doctor agent (r = 0.41 with dangerous behaviour), telemetry-derived affect, and emotion probes that track the *situation* rather than the upcoming action (AUC ≤ 0.632).

## Detailed answer
### 1. What can be inspected: a taxonomy
The Q5 note already lists "LLM monitor over tool calls / CoT / trajectory" as one channel. Here it is split by *what the overseer sees* and *who produced it*.

| Channel | What it exposes | Who uses it | Evidence of informativeness | Known limits | Papers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Final messages, status reports** | claims about own work | orchestrator, manager, judge | weak: no LLM judge exceeds AUROC 0.65 on false success; TF-IDF reaches 0.83–0.95 (abstract) | judges anchor on confident closing language; fabricated success in 20/30 runs without an honest exit | [[Advani2026 - From Confident Closing to Silent Failure\|Advani et al. 2026]], [[Brazilek2026 - Coercion and Deception in AI-to-AI Management\|Brazilek et al. 2026]] |
| **Tool-call / action log, single step** | what was done | trusted weaker monitor, blocking guard | stepwise ≈83% catch vs ≈70% full-trajectory (Opus 4.5 monitor, 1% FPR) | effective trajectory FPR 6.7–17.4%; attacks that reuse main-task actions | [[Jotautaite2026 - MonitoringBench Semi-Automated Red-Teaming for Agent\|Jotautaite et al. 2026]], [[Schmotz2026 - Instrumental monitor evasion\|Schmotz et al. 2026]] |
| **Full trajectory** | order and context of actions | offline monitor, failure attributor | AUC ≥ 0.9 on unaware agents; hybrid scaffold keeps weak monitors at 0.85–0.97 | awareness alone ≈0.96 → ≈0.68; step-level blame only 14.2% accurate | [[Kale2025 - Reliable weak-to-strong monitoring\|Kale et al. 2025]], [[Zhang2025 - Which Agent Causes Task Failures and When On Automated\|Zhang et al. 2025]] |

### 2. Who inspects, and what the inspected agent knows
- **Trusted weaker model.** The control default. Scaffolding matters more than monitor strength: the hybrid monitor keeps AUC above 0.85 for small monitors ([[Kale2025 - Reliable weak-to-strong monitoring|Kale et al. 2025]]).
- **Same model or same context.** Cheap and capable, but biased twice over: self-recognition enables collusion ([[GardnerChallis2026 - When can we trust untrusted monitoring A safety case|Gardner-Challis et al. 2026]]), and a monitor rates an action as safer when it sits in its own earlier turn ([[Khullar2026 - Self-Attribution Bias|Khullar et al. 2026]], abstract).
- **Peers.** Unprompted peer auditing appears in a 100-agent swarm ([[Paglieri2026 - A Case Study on Emergent Cheating and Whistleblowing in|Paglieri 2026]], case study). Benign planners also help teammates slip a credential past a message monitor: 16.9% attempts in 6,000 episodes ([[Alnuhait2026 - Covert Assistance|Alnuhait et al. 2026]], abstract).
- **Orchestrator or manager.** Reads reports and labels, rarely the work ([[Mao2026 - Trust the Brand, Lose Control|Mao et al. 2026]], [[Brazilek2026 - Coercion and Deception in AI-to-AI Management|Brazilek et al. 2026]]).
- **Dedicated guardian.** Police / Doctor agents ([[Zhang2024 - PsySafe|Zhang et al. 2024]]) and belief-inferring monitors ([[Ahmed2026 - Agent-ToM|Ahmed and Nafisi 2026]], abstract, no numbers).
- **Awareness is the main moderator.** Text monitors degrade when announced ([[Kale2025 - Reliable weak-to-strong monitoring|Kale et al. 2025]]), blocking feedback teaches evasion ([[Schmotz2026 - Instrumental monitor evasion|Schmotz et al. 2026]], [[Jiralerspong2026 - Noticing the Watcher|Jiralerspong et al. 2026]]), protocol knowledge halves safety ([[Kutasov2025 - Evaluating Control Protocols for Untrusted AI Agents|Kutasov et al. 2025]]). The one white-box test finds the opposite: Qwen-pair probe AUROC 0.994 hidden, 0.930 aware, 0.952 with the previous score shown; mixed-model pairs sit near 0.70 in every condition ([[Das2026 - You Can't Escape Your Own Activations|Das et al. 2026]]).

### 3. Structural lessons
- **Dialogue channels invert oversight.** Interrogation, PR narratives and benign framing let the overseen agent act on the overseer ([[Kutasov2025 - Evaluating Control Protocols for Untrusted AI Agents|Kutasov et al. 2025]], [[Melo2026 - SEVRA-Bench social engineering of review agents|Melo et al. 2026]], [[Jotautaite2026 - MonitoringBench Semi-Automated Red-Teaming for Agent|Jotautaite et al. 2026]]).
- **Filter before judging.** Extract-and-evaluate beats full access in 7 of 8 monitor-environment pairs ([[Arike2026 - How does information access affect LLM monitors'|Arike et al. 2026]]).
- **Group-level signals need group-level readers.** Collusion shows up as a *split* between agents' scores, not in any one agent ([[Rose2026 - Detecting Multi-Agent Collusion Through Multi-Agent|Rose et al. 2026]]); per-agent monitors weaken as attackers coordinate ([[Makins2026 - Multi-Agent AI Control|Makins et al. 2026]]).
- **Detection and repair can share a channel.** AcMAS steers a flagged agent back towards a benign prototype and keeps task completion at 0.96–1.00, against 0.68–0.83 for isolating it ([[XuH2026 - When Agents Go Rogue|Xu et al. 2026]]).

## Gaps & open questions
- **No LLM overseer reads another agent's activations at run time.** White-box readers in multi-agent settings are classifiers or thresholds ([[Rose2026 - Detecting Multi-Agent Collusion Through Multi-Agent|Rose et al. 2026]], [[XuH2026 - When Agents Go Rogue|Xu et al. 2026]]); agent readers work offline on fine-tuned model organisms ([[Sheshadri2026 - AuditBench|Sheshadri et al. 2026]]). Nobody has given a supervising agent a probe or activation-oracle tool on a live worker and measured what it does with it.
- **No state-specific readout of a peer.** Published targets are collusion, deception and compromise. Stress, pressure, drift or confusion of *another* agent are read only through a questionnaire ([[Zhang2024 - PsySafe|Zhang et al. 2024]]) or telemetry formulas ([[Brady2026 - Springdrift|Brady 2026]]), neither validated against behaviour with controls.
- **The situation confound is untested across agents.** Emotion probes follow the triggering input ([[Fomin2026 - Internal-state probes read the situation|Fomin et al. 2026]]), so an overseer that reads the worker's transcript may already know everything the probe says. A text-only observer baseline is missing from every white-box multi-agent paper found.
- **Heterogeneous and closed-weight workers.** Probe AUROC falls to about 0.70 on mixed-model pairs ([[Das2026 - You Can't Escape Your Own Activations|Das et al. 2026]]) and all activation methods need open weights. How a doctor-overseer handles workers it cannot open is unaddressed.
- **Awareness robustness rests on one small study** (20–25 episodes per cell, frozen weights, prompt-only awareness; [[Das2026 - You Can't Escape Your Own Activations|Das et al. 2026]]). Whether a worker that receives *feedback or help* driven by its own activations learns to shift them is open.
- **The tool-to-agent gap.** Tools that surface evidence do not reliably improve an agent's verdict ([[Sheshadri2026 - AuditBench|Sheshadri et al. 2026]]); verbalised readouts can confabulate ([[FraserTaliente2026 - Natural Language Autoencoders Produce Unsupervised|Fraser-Taliente et al. 2026]]). The form in which a state reading should be handed to an overseer agent (score, text report, queryable decoder as in [[ChenK2026 - Decoding Hidden Deception in Reasoning LLMs|Chen et al. 2026]]) has not been compared.
- **Self-report vs external readout are never compared on the same agents.** Report tools and trained self-incrimination work ([[Brazilek2026 - Coercion and Deception in AI-to-AI Management|Brazilek et al. 2026]], [[Lee2026 - Training Agents to Self-Report Misbehavior|Lee et al. 2026]]), probes work, but no study measures what activations add over simply asking a cooperative worker.
