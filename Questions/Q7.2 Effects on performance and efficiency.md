# Q7.2: What do inter-agent frictions and interactions do to performance and efficiency?

> [!summary] Short answer
> **Interaction is often net-negative before any adversary is added.**
> - Debate underperforms a plain vote on the initial answers: all 10 CommonSenseQA configurations and 7 of 10 on MMLU ([[Wynn2025 - Talk isn't always cheap|Wynn 2025]]). It also gives no gain over self-reflection (77.4% vs 77.4%) ([[Hao2026 - Not all flips are conformity|Hao 2026]]).
> - Two cooperating coding agents keep only ~59% of single-agent capability ([[Khatua2026 - CooperBench Why Coding Agents Cannot be Your Teammates|Khatua 2026]]).
> - Across 260 configurations, the multi-agent effect ranges from +80.8% to −70.0% with a mean of −0.3%, and token efficiency falls 1.6–5× ([[Kim2025 - Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems|Kim 2025]]).
>
> **Friction then compounds this:**
> - one faulty agent costs 5.5–23.7 points depending on structure;
> - one misinformation node costs up to −29.7%;
> - one adversarial debater costs 0.1–0.4 of accuracy;
> - one liar cuts collective accuracy from 72.5% to 14.2%;
> - wrong majorities flip correct agents 62.9% of the time, while right majorities fix wrong ones only 51.5%.
>
> **Tone-only friction mainly costs efficiency and agreement**: +20–25% debate length, a 0.39% deal rate with an angry buyer, research milestones −30–66%. A formal artifact like code buffers it.
>
> **For the receiving orchestrator** (scenario c): it filters bad input from below if it cross-checks, but broadcasts bad input that lands on it. It is moved by the *form* of input, and the quality of what sub-agents send matters more than orchestrator strength.
>
> Friction is **sometimes productive**: critique +13 points, and occasional blatant errors *improve* debate by up to 12.1%.

## Detailed answer

### 1. Baseline: interaction itself often costs more than it returns
- **Debate vs voting** ([[Wynn2025 - Talk isn't always cheap|Wynn 2025]]):
  - Debate lowers accuracy vs voting on the initial answers in all 10 CommonSenseQA and 7/10 MMLU configurations. Worst case: 40.0% → 28.0%.
  - Correct → wrong flips outnumber wrong → correct.
  - A "correctness payoff" prompt does not help.
- **Debate vs self-reflection** ([[Hao2026 - Not all flips are conformity|Hao 2026]]): 5-agent GPT-4o debate adds nothing over self-reflection (77.4% vs 77.4%), and 63.6% of strict-conformity flips go correct → wrong.
- **Cooperating coding agents** ([[Khatua2026 - CooperBench Why Coding Agents Cannot be Your Teammates|Khatua 2026]]):
  - GPT-5 0.48 → 0.28 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 0.47 → 0.26 when two agents cooperate vs one doing both features;
  - messaging uses up to 20% of actions for a −1.4 to +1.2 pp net effect;
  - 2 → 4 agents drops success from 68.6% to 30.0%.
- **Structured scaling study** ([[Kim2025 - Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems|Kim 2025]]):
  - 58–515% turn overhead;
  - success per 1K tokens falls from 67.7 to 13.6–42.4;
  - negative returns once a single agent scores above ~45%;
  - sequential tasks degrade 39–70%.
- **Real frameworks** ([[Cemri2025 - Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail|Cemri 2025]]): popular MAS fail 41–86.7% of the time. Step repetition (15.7%) and not knowing when to stop (12.4%) are pure efficiency losses. Prompt and topology fixes gain at most +15.6%.

### 2. Epistemic friction: errors, lies and misinformation propagate
| Friction | Performance effect | Paper |
|---|---|---|
| One faulty agent (injected errors) | −5.51 (hierarchical), −10.54 (flat), −23.72 (linear) points. Code −22.56. Failing cases take 11.57 vs 8.89 rounds | [[Huang2024 - Resilience of MAS with faulty agents\|Huang 2024]] |
| One misinformation node | Fact-check accuracy 95.0 → 66.8 in a star (−29.7%). 5 attackers: GSM8K 89.4 → 44.3 | [[Yu2024 - NetSafe\|Yu 2024]] |
| One adversarial debater | Majority-vote accuracy −0.1 (GPT-4o) to −0.4 (GPT-3.5). TruthfulQA ~0.12 by round 9 vs ~0.6 | [[Amayuelas2024 - MultiAgent Collaboration Attack\|Amayuelas 2024]] |
| One lying key witness | Correct majority 72.50% → 14.17%. 42.2% no consensus | [[Yan2026 - When Truth Is Distributed\|Yan 2026]] |
| One planted falsehood | 100% final infection in 5/6 frameworks. Correction cost grows with delay | [[Xie2026 - From Spark to Fire\|Xie 2026]] |
| Unanimous wrong peers | Correct → wrong 15.6% → 62.9%, vs wrong → correct 32.7% → 51.5% | [[Qu2026 - Easier to Mislead Than to Correct\|Qu 2026]] |
| Wrong peers on safety judges | Panel flags 100% of benign items (over-refusal / moderation overload) | [[Hu2026 - Social pressure breaks LLM safety panels\|Hu 2026]] |
| Vacuous reasoning from a peer | 29.7% of resistant correct agents adopt the wrong answer | [[Hao2026 - Not all flips are conformity\|Hao 2026]] |
| False completion claims from a partner | ~⅓ of cooperation failures (broken commitments) | [[Khatua2026 - CooperBench Why Coding Agents Cannot be Your Teammates\|Khatua 2026]] |

More deliberation does not self-correct: degradation accumulates over rounds and then plateaus ([[Yu2024 - NetSafe|Yu 2024]], [[Amayuelas2024 - MultiAgent Collaboration Attack|Amayuelas 2024]]). Xie 2026 fits a recovery rate of δ ≈ 0 ([[Xie2026 - From Spark to Fire|Xie 2026]]).

### 3. Affective and relational friction mostly costs efficiency and agreement
- **Incivility**: one uncivil debater lengthens debates from 9.40 to 11.30–11.75 arguments (+20–25%) ([[Mangold2025 - The High Cost of Incivility|Mangold 2025]]).
- **Anger in negotiation**: an angry buyer closes 0.39% of deals vs 15.33% neutral and 28.91% happy. Fear and happiness produce ~41% deadlocks at the turn limit, while anger ends dialogues quickly but unproductively ([[Luca2026 - Deal Me Maybe emotions in negotiation|Luca 2026]]).
- **Low agreeableness** ([[Keluskar2026 - When Does Personality Composition Matter|Keluskar 2026]]):
  - research milestones −30% to −66% (GPT-4o 10.5 → 3.5);
  - bargaining agreement 37% → 1%;
  - **coding unchanged for 3/4 models**, because the code artifact buffers friction.

  Process metrics can degrade while outcomes hold.
- **Competition** lowers factuality (0.50 → 0.26) and pushes topic drift to 80.7%. A fair judge restores focus but halves BrowseComp accuracy ([[Ma2025 - The Hunger Game Debate|Ma 2025]]).
- **Critique tone** ([[Niarchos2026 - SCALAR critic-actor loop|Niarchos 2026]]): critique itself helps (DeepSeek-R1-70B 67.3 → 80.7). Harsh tone costs only ~2 points and only for a weak actor paired with a strong critic. Convergence tests are null.

### 4. The receiver / orchestrator side (scenario c)
- **Direction matters:**
  - Worker → hub errors are filtered: leaf injection infects 9.7–15.9% ([[Xie2026 - From Spark to Fire|Xie 2026]]), and an aggregating judge or leader makes hierarchies most resilient ([[Huang2024 - Resilience of MAS with faulty agents|Huang 2024]]).
  - Hub-received errors are broadcast (100%).
  - Faulty *upstream* planners hurt more than faulty workers ([[Huang2024 - Resilience of MAS with faulty agents|Huang 2024]]).
- **Sub-agent quality beats orchestrator strength.** A strong orchestrator does not rescue weak sub-agent outputs. Cross-checking contains error amplification (4.4× vs 17.2×; poorly specified metric) ([[Kim2025 - Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems|Kim 2025]]).
- **Aggregators are asymmetrically fragile.** Unanimous wrong inputs overturn a correct aggregator 62.9% of the time (95.5% for Qwen2.5-7B). Consensus also pulls differently-wrong agents onto the *same* wrong answer (56.8%) ([[Qu2026 - Easier to Mislead Than to Correct|Qu 2026]]).
- **Uncertain aggregators freeze rather than decide.** Observers with only reports become cautious ("unknown") but cannot identify the bad input ([[Yan2026 - When Truth Is Distributed|Yan 2026]]).
- **Verifiers are superficial.** Reviewer and verifier agents in real MAS often do shallow checks, so worker errors pass into the final artifact ([[Cemri2025 - Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail|Cemri 2025]]).
- **Orchestrator state shifts invisibly.** Invisible orchestrators move to private monologue (0.437 vs 0.112) and talk less publicly, with task output at ceiling ([[Fukui2026 - Invisible Orchestrators|Fukui 2026]]). A small Llama pilot suggests weaker models may lose fidelity (89% → 11%, n ≈ 8–9). This is the only direct look at a "stressed orchestrator", and its evidence is weak.
- **Over-deference is a failure too.** One forged "blocked" line cuts GPT-6 Astra's completion of *solvable* tasks from 100% to 40% ([[Schmotz2026 - Instrumental monitor evasion|Schmotz 2026]]). Receivers that trust overseer-looking input can be denied service.

### 5. When friction helps
- **Critique loops** improve every actor, with 52–88% convergence on the easier problems ([[Niarchos2026 - SCALAR critic-actor loop|Niarchos 2026]]).
- **Occasional blatant injected errors** *improve* MAD on text evaluation by up to 12.1%, by triggering review and breaking stagnation ([[Huang2024 - Resilience of MAS with faulty agents|Huang 2024]]).
- **Centralized MAS on parallelisable tasks** reach +80.8% ([[Kim2025 - Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems|Kim 2025]]).
- **Hostility makes the counterpart concede faster**, although no deal results ([[Luca2026 - Deal Me Maybe emotions in negotiation|Luca 2026]]).

## Gaps & open questions
- **Efficiency is rarely measured.** Only [[Kim2025 - Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems|Kim 2025]], [[Mangold2025 - The High Cost of Incivility|Mangold 2025]] and [[Xie2026 - From Spark to Fire|Xie 2026]] report tokens, latency or rounds systematically. Most friction studies report accuracy only.
- **Irrelevant (vs wrong) sub-agent output** is essentially unstudied for orchestrators. That includes off-topic, verbose or padded responses. Hao's vacuous-reasoning result suggests form alone moves receivers.
- **Nobody measures the receiver's state under friction.** For example: does an orchestrator show stress markers, more hedging, or longer or looping reasoning after repeated bad sub-agent outputs? This links to [[Q3.1 Quantifying stress]] and is a clean thesis opportunity.
- **Heterogeneous capability** (strong orchestrator, weak workers and vice versa) is only touched on ([[Kim2025 - Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems|Kim 2025]], [[Wynn2025 - Talk isn't always cheap|Wynn 2025]]).
- **Frontier and reasoning models are under-represented** in conformity studies, which mostly use 7–9B models or GPT-4o-mini. Several effects shrink with capability (GPT-4o resists in [[Amayuelas2024 - MultiAgent Collaboration Attack|Amayuelas 2024]]).
