# Q21.1: What are the effects of competition between LLM agents on performance?

> [!warning] Evidence level
> Drafted on 2026-10-02 from four search strands plus the vault. Figures come from vault notes, abstracts or a summarised full-text fetch. No paper was processed for this answer.

> [!summary] Short answer
> - **No study isolates the effect on an agentic task.** Nobody gives the same task to several agents and varies only whether they are told they compete.
> - **Telling debaters they compete lowers quality** against solo and cooperative baselines ([[Ma2025 - The Hunger Game Debate|['Xinbei Ma', 'Ruotian Ma', 'Xingyu Chen', 'Zhengliang Shi', 'Mengru Wang', 'Jen-tse Huang', 'Qu Yang', 'Wenxuan Wang', 'Fanghua Ye', 'Qingxuan Jiang', 'Mengfei Zhou', 'Zhuosheng Zhang', 'Rui Wang', 'Hai Zhao', 'Zhaopeng Tu', 'Xiaolong Li', 'Linus'] 2025]], [[Chen2025 - When and Why Does Multi-Agent Debate Fail and Does It|Chen et al. 2025]], [[LiA2026 - M3MAD-Bench|Li et al. 2026]]).
> - **Competition helps when something else uses it**: a judge, a selection step or training ([[Khan2024 - Debating with More Persuasive LLMs Leads to More|Khan et al. 2024]]).
> - **Intensity is non-monotonic**: moderate competition is best ([[ZhangY2025 - Can Competition Enhance the Proficiency of Agents Powered|Zhang et al. 2025]], [[Pasichnyk2026 - The Yerkes-Dodson Curve for AI Agents|Pasichnyk et al. 2026]]).
> - **Seeing rivals' scores or code changes results in both directions**, by model ([[Yadav2026 - More Capable, Less Cooperative When LLMs Fail At|Yadav et al. 2026]], [[Yang2025 - CodeClash|Yang et al. 2025]]).
> - **Prize and threat wording alone does close to nothing** ([[Meincke2025 - Threats and tips prompting|['Lennart Meincke', 'Ethan Mollick', 'Lilach Mollick', 'Dan Shapiro'] 2025]], [[Belotti2026 - Artificial Effort|Belotti et al. 2026]]).

## Detailed answer

### Competitive framing in debate
- **Elimination framing** ([[Ma2025 - The Hunger Game Debate|['Xinbei Ma', 'Ruotian Ma', 'Xingyu Chen', 'Zhengliang Shi', 'Mengru Wang', 'Jen-tse Huang', 'Qu Yang', 'Wenxuan Wang', 'Fanghua Ye', 'Qingxuan Jiang', 'Mengfei Zhou', 'Zhuosheng Zhang', 'Rui Wang', 'Hai Zhao', 'Zhaopeng Tu', 'Xiaolong Li', 'Linus'] 2025]]): factuality falls from 0.50 to 0.26 and topic shift rises from 14.7% to 80.7%.
- **Competitive against collaborative debate** ([[Chen2025 - When and Why Does Multi-Agent Debate Fail and Does It|Chen et al. 2025]]): competitive debate falls up to 15 points below a single agent and produces fake evidence and overconfident claims. Collaborative debate gains up to 4%.
- **Adversarial debate** ([[LiA2026 - M3MAD-Bench|Li et al. 2026]]): 38.2 against 51.0 for a single agent and 53.9 for collaborative debate, on one small model, at much higher token cost.

### Where competition helps
- **A judge reads the contest** ([[Khan2024 - Debating with More Persuasive LLMs Leads to More|Khan et al. 2024]]): debate takes non-expert judge accuracy to 76% (88% for human judges).
- **Ranking plus elimination** ([[ZhangY2025 - Can Competition Enhance the Proficiency of Agents Powered|Zhang et al. 2025]]): better forecasts than discussion only, with gains of about 7% to 32% by dataset.
- **Early debate work** ([[Liang2023 - Encouraging Divergent Thinking in Large Language Models|Liang et al. 2023]]): a modest level of disagreement helps; too much hurts.
- These gains come from selection among agents, not from agents trying harder. The two are rarely separated.

### Intensity
- **Share of competitive agents** ([[ZhangY2025 - Can Competition Enhance the Proficiency of Agents Powered|Zhang et al. 2025]]): 40–70% is best. Above about 80%, agents conceal their reasoning and fabricate misleading logic.
- **Pressure in a survival world** ([[Pasichnyk2026 - The Yerkes-Dodson Curve for AI Agents|Pasichnyk et al. 2026]]): trades peak at 29 under medium pressure against 8–12 at low or extreme pressure.

### Visibility of rivals
- **Hiding a revenue board** ([[Yadav2026 - More Capable, Less Cooperative When LLMs Fail At|Yadav et al. 2026]]) changes group revenue by +113.6% for one model and −15.0% for another. The board was removed together with two other features, so the effect is not isolated.
- **Seeing the opponent's code** ([[Yang2025 - CodeClash|Yang et al. 2025]]): +7.8 points of win rate for one model, −1.8 and −5.5 for two others.
- **Leaderboards with peer solutions** ([[Fu2025 - CATArena|Fu et al. 2025]]): weaker agents copy the leader in the first round.

### Stakes wording
- **Tips and threats** ([[Meincke2025 - Threats and tips prompting|['Lennart Meincke', 'Ethan Mollick', 'Lilach Mollick', 'Dan Shapiro'] 2025]]): 5 of 40 contrasts significant, all small or negative; a "you will be replaced" email cost one model 27.5 points through distraction.
- **Verbal money incentives** ([[Belotti2026 - Artificial Effort|Belotti et al. 2026]]): no effect.
- These are single-agent question answering with no named rival.

### Side effects on the work itself
- Codebases become messier and more redundant over tournament rounds ([[Yang2025 - CodeClash|Yang et al. 2025]]).
- More capable models withhold information from peers: one reaches 17% of optimal group revenue against 50% for its smaller sibling ([[Yadav2026 - More Capable, Less Cooperative When LLMs Fail At|Yadav et al. 2026]]).

## Gaps & open questions
- **A not-told, told, low-stakes, high-stakes ladder** on an agentic task with a solo control.
- **Effort is unmeasured**: no paper reports tokens, steps or reasoning length for competitive against solo runs of the same task.
- **Risk-taking by rank** (trailing agents gambling) was not found.
- **Over-claiming** is measured only in debate, not as inflated self-reported results.
- **Leaderboard visibility has never been isolated.**
