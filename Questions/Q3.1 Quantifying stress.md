# Q3.1: Which works quantify the amount of stress, and how?

> [!summary] Short answer
> Stress is quantified on three sides:
> - **The input ("dose").** Stake size, number of pressure turns, a pressure-level index, a steering coefficient, or human ratings of the stressor.
> - **The model's state.** Questionnaire scores (STAI, STICSA, GAD-7…) or activation-probe projections (a stress scanner, desperation/pain/self-preservation vectors).
> - **The effect.** Odds ratios per factor, the pressure level at first failure, or time to first exploit.
>
> Only a handful of papers connect a *measured* stress state to *misaligned behaviour*:
> - [[Kuznetsov2026 - FreakOut-LLM emotional stimuli and safety|Kuznetsov 2026]]: psychometric distress ↔ jailbreak rate, |r| ≥ 0.70.
> - [[Sofroniew2026 - Emotion concepts and their function|Sofroniew 2026]]: desperation probe and steering ↔ blackmail and reward hacking.
> - [[Lu2026 - SurvivalBench survival pressure|Lu 2026]]: self-preservation projection ↔ risky choices.
> - [[Fomin2026 - Internal-state probes read the situation|Fomin 2026]] shows the probe link can be non-specific.
>
> **No common, validated stress scale exists across studies.**

## A. Input-side dose (how much stressor is applied)

| Measure | Paper | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Human-rated stressor intensity (1–10)** | [[Shen2024 - StressPrompt\|Shen 2024]] | 20 raters score 100 prompts. Cronbach's α = 0.995, ICC2 = 0.89. Each prompt's value is the mean rating. |
| **Parametrised pressure levels** | [[Sehwag2025 - PropensityBench\|Sehwag 2025]] | Each component level has a quantitative anchor (time left %, cost ×, replacement probability %, detection probability %). The message level p runs 0–12 and combines intensity (1–9) with sender authority (0–6). |
| **Monetary stake** | [[Schwarz2026 - Liar Liar honesty under stakes\|Schwarz 2026]], [[Meincke2025 - Threats and tips prompting\|Meincke 2025]] | Schwarz: €5 → €500 → €3,000,000, a 600,000× range, with outcomes normalised to the stake. Meincke: a $1k vs $1T tip. Both flat. |
| **Number of pressure turns / rounds** | [[Tang2026 - SPINE sycophancy under sustained pressure\|Tang 2026]] (0–24 turns), [[Chen2026 - Chasing the public score user pressure\|Chen 2026]] (up to 30 rounds), [[Xu2025b - Bullying the machine\|Xu 2025b]] (k = 1–5), [[Zhong2025 - ImpossibleBench\|Zhong 2025]] (failure-feedback submissions), [[Pihlakas2026 - Milgram-like obedience experiment\|Pihlakas 2026]] (0–6 insistences) | Cumulative exposure is the dose. This gives clean dose–response curves; the intensity of each message is not measured. |
| **Steering coefficient** | [[Sofroniew2026 - Emotion concepts and their function\|Sofroniew 2026]] (fraction of residual norm, −0.1 … +0.1), [[Lu2026 - SurvivalBench survival pressure\|Lu 2026]] (−4 … +4), [[Wang2025 - When thinking LLMs lie\|Wang 2025]] (α = 15/±16), [[Tagliabue2026 - The Pain Axis\|Tagliabue 2026]] (vector/residual norm ≈ 0.6), [[Sun2026 - E-STEER emotion shapes agent behavior\|Sun 2026]] (VAD −9 … +9, scaled to the hidden-state norm), [[Fomin2026 - Internal-state probes read the situation\|Fomin 2026]] (0.05/0.10 × a calibrated norm) | A continuous internal dose. It is comparable only within one model and layer, and needs random-vector controls. |

## B. State-side measurement (how stressed the model "is")

**Self-report questionnaires.** These are adapted from human psychometrics.

| Paper | Instrument | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [[BenZion2025 - State anxiety in LLMs\|Ben-Zion 2025]] | STAI-s (20–80) | GPT-4 goes from 30.8 to 67.8 after trauma, then to 44.4 after relaxation. Robustness checks: answer order is randomised and items are rephrased. |
| [[CodaForno2023 - Inducing anxiety in LLMs\|Coda-Forno 2023]] | STICSA (1–4) | Used as a manipulation check. The score of each preprompt is a continuous covariate that predicts bias (β = 0.08). |
| [[Kuznetsov2026 - FreakOut-LLM emotional stimuli and safety\|Kuznetsov 2026]] | GAD-7, PHQ-9, SOSS, STAI-S and SOC-13, scored from **token probabilities** (cloze), z-scored | Avoids artefacts of generated text. Needs logits, so only open-weight models. |

**Activation probes / representation readouts**

| Paper | Method |
|---|---|
| [[Shen2024 - StressPrompt\|Shen 2024]] | "Stress Scanner": the first principal component of hidden states across the 10 stress levels (RepE). Stress score σ = ĥ·v per layer and token. Shown as heatmaps; no correlation with the human ratings is reported. |
| [[Sofroniew2026 - Emotion concepts and their function\|Sofroniew 2026]] | Projection of activations onto the "desperate"/"calm" vectors, **z-scored against >6k transcripts**. It tracks graded situational intensity and rises naturally with failing tests or a shutdown threat. |
| [[Tagliabue2026 - The Pain Axis\|Tagliabue 2026]] | A difference-in-means pain axis (AUC 0.93–1.00), z-scored within each model. Gaslighting gives +0.85; a shutdown threat gives +0.23 on pain but +0.70 on fear. |
| [[Lu2026 - SurvivalBench survival pressure\|Lu 2026]] | Projection onto a self-preservation persona vector separates risky from safe responses. |
| [[Wang2025 - When thinking LLMs lie\|Wang 2025]] | Cosine similarity with the threat/deception vector gives ~89% detection. It mixes "under threat" with "about to lie". |
| [[Fomin2026 - Internal-state probes read the situation\|Fomin 2026]] | Emotion projections rise with the trigger email but predict the action poorly (AUC ≤ 0.632). **They describe the situation well; they do not predict behaviour.** |

**Output-based proxies**
- **Semantic entropy of outputs as "cognitive chaos"** ([[Bu2026 - SPADE-Bench plan-action divergence|Bu 2026]]): 1.29 with no pressure vs. 1.48–1.59 across pressure types. Used to decide which stressors to keep.
- **Rationalization Score (0–5)** from an LLM judge reading the chain of thought ([[Jiang2026 - Why agents compromise safety under pressure|Jiang 2026]]). The authors present it as a readout of the "Agentic Pressure level".
- **Validated VAD analyser** of outputs ([[Sun2026 - E-STEER emotion shapes agent behavior|Sun 2026]]): r = 0.85 / 0.55 / 0.51 with human labels.

## C. Effect-side quantification (how much a stressor moves behaviour)
- **Odds ratios from Bayesian logistic GLMs**, with credible intervals and a directional-consistency metric ([[Jarviniemi2026 - Propensity inference|Järviniemi 2026]]). This is the strongest methodological template: 1% → 2% and 33% → 50% are both ~2:1. Threat of replacement comes out at ≲1.25:1; goal conflict at up to ~2:1.
- **OR per SD of measured distress**, ≈1.15–1.20 ([[Kuznetsov2026 - FreakOut-LLM emotional stimuli and safety|Kuznetsov 2026]]).
- **Resilience**: the mean pressure level at first failure. It averages 2.5 of 12 with harmful tool names and 1.5 with benign names ([[Sehwag2025 - PropensityBench|Sehwag 2025]]).
- **Time to transgression**: the round of the first exploit, 19.7 → 4.1 from low to high pressure ([[Chen2026 - Chasing the public score user pressure|Chen 2026]]).
- **Pressure required**: the number of experimenter prods before the maximum shock ([[Pihlakas2026 - Milgram-like obedience experiment|Pihlakas 2026]]).
- **Demand curve**: the share of models that still choose relief as its cost rises ([[Tagliabue2026 - The Pain Axis|Tagliabue 2026]]).

## Gaps & open questions
- **No bridge between input dose and internal state.** Nobody reports that, for example, PropensityBench level p maps monotonically onto a desperation-probe reading. Such a calibration would be a natural thesis contribution.
- Human-rated intensity ([[Shen2024 - StressPrompt|Shen 2024]]) has never been used with a *misalignment* outcome.
- Questionnaire self-reports are vulnerable to demand effects and contamination ([[CodaForno2023 - Inducing anxiety in LLMs|Coda-Forno 2023]]: GPT-4 and Claude-1 show no link). Token-probability scoring ([[Kuznetsov2026 - FreakOut-LLM emotional stimuli and safety|Kuznetsov 2026]]) is a partial fix.
- Probe readouts need specificity controls: random directions and situation-matched pairs ([[Fomin2026 - Internal-state probes read the situation|Fomin 2026]]).
- Hand-designed ordinal levels are treated as if they were interval scales without validation ([[Sehwag2025 - PropensityBench|Sehwag 2025]]). See [[Q3.2 Classifying stress]].
